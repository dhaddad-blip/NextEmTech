AI Governance, Ethics & Audit Readiness

Human oversight you can prove — not just promise.

Regulatory pressure on AI is moving from guidance to enforcement. The EU AI Act begins imposing real penalties on high-risk AI systems from August 2026. ISO/IEC 42001, the international AI management standard, is increasingly showing up as a procurement requirement. Boards and PMOs are being asked a question most can't yet answer with evidence: when an AI agent made a recommendation, who reviewed it, and what did they decide?

This is where CPMAI discipline meets governance — not a policy document, but a working system that makes AI oversight visible, timestamped, and reviewable.

What we do

Five disciplines, one goal: AI your organization can trust — and prove it trusts responsibly.

G

Governance

Decision rights, policies, and oversight structures that make clear who can approve what an AI agent recommends — defined before deployment, not reconstructed after an incident.

E

Ethics

Fairness, transparency, and avoiding harm built into how recommendations are reviewed and acted on — not a values statement bolted on after the system ships.

R

Responsible AI

AI applied where it genuinely adds value, with explicit limits on what it shouldn't decide alone. Always advisory over consequential decisions — never autonomous.

D

Data Quality

The CPMAI-aligned discipline of understanding, auditing, and validating the data an AI system learns from — before anyone trusts what it recommends.

A

Audits

A timestamped, tamper-evident record of every AI recommendation and every human decision — ready to show a regulator, a board, or a client, not assembled after the fact.

Proof, not just a framework

Most AI governance work stops at a document. Ours started as a working system: the Project Operations Digital Twin fuses project telemetry and AI-generated analysis into a single command center — where every AI call is stateless, every recommendation is logged, and a human reviews it before any action is taken.

AI provides the analysis. The person owns the decision.

That's not a slogan. It's the audit trail, running today.
